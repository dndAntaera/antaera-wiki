# Overview

For travelers used to chaos in the stars—planets on broken orbits, spheres full of ruin and madness—the Antæra Sphere comes as something else entirely. Familiar. Grounded. It doesn’t shout for your attention with bizarre magic or roaring suns. Instead, it feels like stepping outside on a cool morning and finding the world just as you left it. There’s a kind of peace here, like everything is moving at its own pace, in balance. Seasons come and go the way they should. The stars don’t change their minds. People live, die, build things, grow old. The sky doesn’t scream. Sailors call it the “breathing sphere,” not because it’s calm, but because it feels alive—and aware.

At its center is Solion, a golden-white star that burns warm and steady. Not too bright. Not too distant. Its light has fed the worlds for longer than anyone can say, and it doesn’t seem to be in a hurry to change. Solion doesn’t flare or rage like some of the monstrous suns in other systems. It simply shines, every day, like it always has. Some priests claim it’s blessed by the Positive Plane. Others think it’s a relic, older than time itself. Whatever it is, the people who live under it trust it in a way that’s rare among the stars. You don’t worship Solion—you depend on it, like breath or blood.

The planets here follow simple tracks. The inner worlds are harsh but not strange—Vireen, alive with fire and ruled by magma dragons, and Caelion, a sun-blasted desert where giant worms swim through sand. Then there’s the middle belt, where Antæra and Thalmyra sit. Antæra is full of life—forests, oceans, cities, all sorts of people from who-knows-where. No one remembers where they came from, but they’ve made something beautiful. Thalmyra is wetter, wilder, run by elves who shape their cities out of living trees. Past them, things get colder. Norys is a frozen plateau world wrapped in storms, and its moon, Serathos, is worse—a silent glacier hiding something no one’s quite figured out. And out on the edge is Myrelune, a shattered husk of lightning and ash, shaking itself apart one storm at a time.

But it’s what’s buried under Serathos that draws the whispers: the Althuun Prism. Tan starmetal, covered in strange symbols, glowing under the ice. The machines that guard it never speak. They just move, slow and certain, as if they’ve been walking since before the gods had names. No one knows what the Prism is. Some say it holds the sphere together. Others say it’s not holding anything at all—it’s waiting.

And then there’s Antæra itself—the planet, not the sphere. There are cities hidden in its mountains, sealed tight and untouched by time. Walls made of smooth metal that doesn’t rust, doors that don’t open, symbols no one can read. The people don’t talk about them much, but they know they’re there. The name “Antæra” is carved into places that no one alive built. It's not just a name—it’s a reminder. That something came before. That someone remembered enough to leave these ruins behind.

# Planetology & Astrology

- Type of Sphere: Standard
- Primary Body:
    - Name: Solion
    - Type: Fire Body (Golden-white star)
- Spelljamming Aware: No

## Inner Track (<200 million miles)

- **Planet Name**: Vireen
    - **Distance**: 85 million miles
    - **Size Category**: C
    - **Planet Type**: Fire type
    - **Planet Shape**: Spherical
    - **Other Notes**: Vireen is a searing volcanic world, its surface wracked by endless eruptions and rivers of glowing magma. Towering calderas split open like festering wounds, and the skies are thick with choking clouds of ash and toxic volcanic gases. The atmosphere is nearly unbreathable to non-native life, burning the lungs and eyes of any unprotected visitor. Amid this inferno, power reigns supreme. The world is ruled by ancient magma dragons and their red-scaled cousins, each claiming dominion over smoking mountain fortresses. Legions of kobold servants dwell in obsidian tunnels and lava-slick warrens, building and maintaining the vast lairs and treasuries of their draconic masters. Vireen is not uninhabited—only unforgiving.

- **Planet Name**: Caelion
    - **Distance**: 127 million miles
    - **Size Category**: D
    - **Planet Type**: Earth type
    - **Planet Shape**: Spherical
    - **Other Notes**: Caelion is an unforgiving desert world of sun-scorched dunes and blinding salt flats, where temperatures soar by day and plummet by night. Life survives only in deep ravines and jagged canyons, where rare springs feed hidden oases bursting with resilient flora and fiercely territorial fauna. These lush biomes form narrow ribbons of green threading through the vast arid wasteland. The open sands, however, belong to ancient terrors—titanic sand worms that swim through the dunes like water, drawn to the slightest tremor. Many have tried to settle this world, lured by its rare resources and strange beauty, only to meet their fates in the belly of a sand worm. Caelion is as beautiful as it is deadly.

## Goldilocks Zone (200–300 million miles)

- **Planet Name**: Antæra
    - **Distance**: 247 million miles
    - **Size Category**: E
    - **Planet Type**: Earth type
    - **Planet Shape**: Spherical
    - **Other Notes**: Antæra is a world of deep forests, rolling oceans, and jagged mountains—but its true wonder lies in the complexity of its people. Civilizations here are varied and vibrant, made up of countless mortal races that appear to have originated from distant spheres. Elves, dwarves, goliaths, halflings, and stranger folk all share the land, yet no one truly knows how or why they came to be here. The most ancient ruins whisper of a unified age long past, forgotten even by the gods. In recent generations, one human kingdom was partially uplifted—gifted a surge of knowledge in metallurgy, energy, and computation that far outpaced their peers. No war followed, no conquest—only the quiet construction of a single massive colony ship, which was launched in secret into the void beyond the stars. The rest of the world remained untouched. Some claim this act was guided by agents from the Malifortis Sphere, though no proof remains, only rumor and a string of vanished scholars.

- **Planet Name**: Thalmyra
    - **Distance**: 266 million miles
    - **Size Category**: D
    - **Planet Type**: Water type
    - **Planet Shape**: Spherical
    - **Other Notes**: Though dominated by vast oceans, Thalmyra boasts a breathtaking variety of ecosystems. Isolated archipelagos and floating forest-isles rise from the sea, each one teeming with unique flora and fauna. Beneath the waves lie glowing caverns, deep trenches, and shallow reefs filled with life. The surface is scattered with warm plains, misty jungles, and mountainous islands shaped by old magic. At the heart of this world rules a proud and ancient elven dynasty, its cities grown rather than built, blending seamlessly into the landscape. Thalmyra is a sister world to Antæra in spirit—more wild, more waterbound, but no less alive.

## Outer Track (>300 million miles)

- **Planet Name**: Norys
    - **Distance**: 312 million miles
    - **Size Category**: C
    - **Planet Type**: Water type
    - **Planet Shape**: Spherical
    - **Other Notes**: Norys is a harsh, wind-scoured tundra world cloaked in snow and mist. Its landscape is dominated by towering plateaus—some miles wide—that rise like titanic steps through the clouds. The surface is locked in a permanent winter, with biting winds and near-constant snowfall. On the worst days, razor-sharp freezing rain scours the land, slicing through hide, cloth, and steel alike, and layering the terrain in brittle ice. Travel between regions is treacherous, as sudden blizzards and icefalls can bury landmarks within minutes. The silence between storms is absolute, broken only by the distant crack of ice or the howl of something moving where nothing should be.

- **Planet Name**: Serathos
    - **Distance**: 312 million miles
    - **Size Category**: D
    - **Planet Type**: Water type
    - **Planet Shape**: Spherical
    - **Other Notes**: Serathos, the moon of Norys, is a lifeless orb of glacial ice, its surface fractured and slick with centuries of frozen accumulation. The atmosphere is thin, toxic, and wholly unbreathable by natural life. No flora or fauna endure here. Yet Serathos is not unguarded—ancient constructs roam its icy plains and cratered ridges, moving with patient precision. Believed to be remnants of the Ancient Antæran civilization, these machines are thought to be eternal wardens of the Althuun Prism hidden deep beneath the surface. They do not speak, do not age, and do not permit intrusion.

- **Planet Name**: Myrelune
    - **Distance**: 389 million miles
    - **Size Category**: E
    - **Planet Type**: Earth type
    - **Planet Shape**: Spherical
    - **Other Notes**: Myrelune is a lifeless ball of rock and dust, its cracked surface swept bare by abrasive winds. The atmosphere is thick with suspended grit and particulate ash, making it entirely unbreathable. There is no water, no plant life, and no evidence of habitation. Towering clouds churn endlessly above the terrain, generating constant lightning storms that race across the horizon. These electrical surges ignite dust into firestorms and shatter stone into glass, rendering the planet violently uninhabitable. Whatever secrets Myrelune once held are long lost beneath its storm-wracked shell.
