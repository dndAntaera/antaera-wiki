# Overview

Travelers entering the Inundatio Sphere often describe an eerie kind of quiet—not the silence of death, but something more detached, more ambient. There’s a disorienting sense of stillness that creeps into the bones after just a few days. Things feel slow here: sound, movement, even the passage of time. Spelljammer crews swap stories about clocks running strange, or conversations that seem to echo in their heads long after they’re done. It’s not hostile, just unnerving in its consistency. Veteran sailors watch the stars more than the instruments, and many insist on rotating watches even during calm stretches, claiming the sphere has a way of making you drift—mentally and otherwise.

At the center of this system burns Nai’trael, a fire body with an unusually soft glow. It doesn’t radiate like most stars—its light is steady, faintly golden, and diffused across the void like lamplight behind gauze. Despite its gentle nature, it somehow sustains the entire sphere, warming even the outer planets enough to keep their oceans liquid or their storms turning. Some believe the star is bound to an elemental or divine source rather than ordinary fusion; others think it’s a fragment of a greater power long since gone. Either way, Nai’trael acts less like a blazing sun and more like a slow-burning heart, pulsing softly at the center of everything.

All of the known planets in the Inundatio Sphere share a single trait—they are water worlds. Some boil and burst like Mirael, whose violent eruptions fill the sky with superheated mist. Others are calm but strange, like Charhessa, where the surface feels more like thick gel than liquid. Cyrintha, resting in the Goldilocks Zone, is a temperate world of scattered islands and ancient sea-faring cultures. Farther out, Voruneth lies frozen beneath a thick crust of ancient ice, while Kuraali orbits even farther as two twin spheres joined by a shimmering arc of vapor. Together, they form a kind of pattern—oceans in motion, frozen, bonded, or boiling, held in balance by Nai’trael’s subtle light and distant warmth.

Spelljammers passing through the system often seek out its stranger features. The most infamous is the Halo of Thaloruun, a massive rotating ring of tan-colored metal that floats near the edge of the Goldilocks Zone. It’s widely believed to be a relic of the Antæran civilization, though no one’s been able to prove it. What’s clear is that it steadily discharges water from some unknown source, which freezes the moment it hits wildspace, forming a belt of sharp, jagged ice known as Thaloruun’s Spill. A Mercane-run mining guild operates in the belt, harvesting rare fire planar crystals using spelljamming ships fitted with repulsion fields to avoid accidental explosions. Despite the dangers, it remains one of the more profitable—if volatile—trade routes in the sphere.

Far beyond the ecliptic drifts the Cloister of Silent Echoes, a ruined monastery encased in a perfect globe of suspended seawater. The structure inside appears whole, untouched by time or decay, and on quiet days, passing crews claim to hear faint chanting through the void. No one has breached the barrier—not with spells, weapons, or divine rites. Whether it’s sealed by the will of those within or by some long-lost magic, no one can say. Nearby, half-lost in the ice fields of the Spill, floats the Anhydral Lens—a spiral-shaped construct of tan Antæran starmetal. It doesn’t emit light or magic, but its presence distorts time, sound, and thought. Captains who pass too close report strange auditory mirages, memory loops, and a sensation of something vast and unfinished, watching just beyond reach.

# Planetology & Astrology

- Type of Sphere: Standard
- Primary Body:
    - Name: Nai’trael
    - Type: Fire Body (Pale, Luminous Star)
- Spelljamming Aware: Yes

## Inner Track (<200 million miles)

- **Planet Name**: Mirael
    - **Distance**: 80 million miles
    - **Size Category**: D
    - **Planet Type**: Water
    - **Shape**: Spherical
    - **Other Notes**: Mirael is a world in constant upheaval. Towering geysers burst from its boiling surface, scattering flame and scalding vapor into the skies. Chains of jagged islands rise from the waves, marked by volcanic springs and incandescent mist. Black and bronze dragons battle endlessly for control of the archipelagos, while Water Genasi tribes dwell on the isles, worshipping the dragons as living gods. Beneath the surface, the boiling seas are ruled by ancient sea and aquatic dragons, who claim the depths with cold and silent dominion.

- **Planet Name**: Charhessa
    - **Distance**: 145 million miles
    - **Size Category**: E
    - **Planet Type**: Water
    - **Shape**: Amorphous
    - **Other Notes**: Charhessa doesn’t ripple or churn like most oceans. Instead, it pulses—slowly, unnervingly—like something alive. Its waters are thick with mineral sludge and alchemical runoff, swirling in iridescent layers that distort sound and light. The surface is dotted with strange, rust-streaked industrial complexes rising from isolated islands—silent, half-sunken ruins of some forgotten age. Scholars believe these facilities once mined or processed something deep within the planet, and that their unknown creators abandoned Charhessa when it began to collapse under its own corruption. Now, the depths are ruled by aberrations—eyeless, psionic predators that move through the polluted sea with dreadful grace, hunting by vibration and psionic detection.

## Goldilocks Zone (200–300 million miles)

- **Planet Name**: Cyrintha
    - **Distance**: 240 million miles
    - **Size Category**: H
    - **Planet Type**: Water
    - **Shape**: Spherical
    - **Other Notes**: Cyrintha is the heart of the sphere, a temperate oceanic world dotted with wide archipelagos and scattered island continents formed from stone, soil, and volcanic ridges. Its warm currents and starlit bays are home to ancient seafaring elven cultures and diverse clans of hengeyokai, who navigate by constellation and tide. The peoples of Cyrintha are deeply spiritual, interpreting the sea’s moods and the stars’ alignments as divine guidance. Tome dragons are venerated as sacred advisors and living repositories of wisdom, while Hex dragons are feared as walking disasters—portents of imbalance and punishment. Every journey is undertaken with prayer, and even storms are met with reverence rather than resistance.

- **Planet Name**: Thaloruun’s Spill
    - **Distance**: 275 million miles
    - **Size Category**: Varies
    - **Planet Type**: Belt
    - **Shape**: Belt
    - **Other Notes**: This glittering belt of ice chunks wasn’t always here. It formed slowly, over millennia, as cold water from the Elemental Plane of Water spilled endlessly from the relic known as the Halo of Thaloruun. As the liquid hit the vacuum of wildspace, it froze into glinting shards and tumbling monoliths, building a slow, spiraling ring of danger and wonder. Spelljammers who brave the belt speak of ghost currents, whispering breezes where there should be none, and frozen shapes just beneath the surface—statues, they say, or something older.

## Outer Track (>300 million miles)

- **Planet Name**: Voruneth
    - **Distance**: 340 million miles
    - **Size Category**: H
    - **Planet Type**: Water
    - **Shape**: Elliptical
    - **Other Notes**: Voruneth is a meat grinder of a world—a brutal, frozen leviathan adrift in the void. Its partially frozen oceans are choked with jagged ice floes, each as sharp as forged blades, grinding together with the force of tides and cracking through hulls like paper. The cold is absolute, the skies slate gray, and the silence is broken only by the groaning of shifting ice and the occasional, thunderous surfacing of the planet’s only inhabitants: monstrous sea beasts with hide like adamantine, ancient and instinctual, who tear through anything that dares enter their domain. There are no cities here, no prayers—only survival.

- **Planet Name**: Kuraali
    - **Distance**: 420 million miles
    - **Size Category**: E
    - **Planet Type**: Water
    - **Shape**: Cluster
    - **Other Notes**: Kuraali is a loose cluster of twin water worlds and satellite islets, all revolving around an enormous manifest zone to the Elemental Plane of Water that churns with impossible tides and luminous mist. The entire cluster orbits the system’s primary like a drifting gyre. At the heart of the cluster, a vaporous arc pulses between the two largest bodies, binding them in a ceaseless exchange of energy and current. Naturally forming portals appear and vanish within the arc, allowing passage between the twin worlds without crossing the intervening space. The inhabitants are thought to be part of a singular, sprawling civilization that exists on both sides of the manifest zone—one side on the material plane, the other submerged in the timeless depths of the Elemental Plane of Water, built above ancient ruins that stretch into the dark.
